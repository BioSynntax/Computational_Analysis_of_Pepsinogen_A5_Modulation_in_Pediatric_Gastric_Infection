# Computational_Analysis_of_Pepsinogen_A5_Modulation_in_Pediatric_Gastric_Infection
In silico computational analysis of Pepsinogen A5, including protein modeling, structural validation, activation-state preparation, molecular docking, ligand–protein interaction analysis, and ADME evaluation to investigate potential modulators relevant to pediatric gastric infection.

## Abstract

### Introduction

Pepsin is produced as an inactive form, pepsinogen, to avoid gastric autodigestion and maintain regulated enzymatic function in the stomach. Uncontrolled pepsin activation has been implicated in pediatric gastric disorders, including gastritis, peptic ulcers, and infection by *Helicobacter pylori*. Understanding the activation of pepsinogen and its interaction with bioactive ligands may provide a basis for targeted therapeutic intervention in pediatric gastric disease.

### Methods

A three-dimensional model of human Pepsinogen A5 was generated using MODELLER. Activation of the model involved removal of the first 44 amino acids, protonation of residues at pH 2, and energy minimization. Molecular docking of five phytocompounds was performed using AutoDock, and ligand–protein interactions were analyzed using Discovery Studio. Conserved sequence patterns and protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks were investigated using MEME Suite, STRING, and Cytoscape.

### Results

The selected phytocompounds demonstrated favorable predicted binding energies: Decursin (-9.60 kcal/mol), Catechin (-9.20 kcal/mol), Quercetin (-9.19 kcal/mol), Irisolidone (-8.90 kcal/mol), and Glabridin (-7.61 kcal/mol). Predicted ligand interactions involved residues including TRP 57, MET 47, ILE 58, PHE 44, PHE 18, LEU 59, PHE 63, GLN 45, GLY 43, and VAL 62. Motif analysis identified conserved sequence regions potentially associated with protein function, while PPI analysis indicated relationships with digestive enzymes and gastric regulatory proteins.

### Conclusion

This integrated in silico analysis provides insights into Pepsinogen A5 activation, structural characteristics, and potential ligand-mediated modulation. The findings support further investigation of selected phytochemicals as putative modulators of pepsinogen/pepsin activity in pediatric gastric disorders, including gastritis and *Helicobacter pylori*-associated conditions.
